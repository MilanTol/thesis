1. GENERAL INFORMATION

Project or Paper Title : Probing substructure with cosmic shear

Project or Paper summary/short description:

Upcoming weak lensing surveys will measure cosmic shear down to sub-arcminute scales with percent-level precision. We identify which effects contribute most significantly to the signal, clarifying what the scientific community must constrain in order to properly interpret the observations. An observable of interest in this regime is the abundance of low-mass haloes and subhaloes: a key prediction of cold dark matter, where a suppression in this abundance could hint at warm dark matter theories. 

To this end, we use a halo model that incorporates substructure to capture the effects of subhaloes on weak lensing, and account for baryonic physics through a sensitivity analysis of the gas and stellar model parameters. We find that cosmic shear at angular separations of $20$--$60$ arcseconds is most sensitive to the parameters governing the outskirts of the gas profile in cluster-scale haloes, while the stellar contribution is a second-order effect most present at scales of $3$--$8$ arcseconds, with its scatter being smaller than expected survey precision. Finally, we find the signal to be sufficiently sensitive to subhalo abundance that, given current constraints on the gas profile and sufficiently strong constraints on the subhalo distribution within parent haloes, warm dark matter could in principle be discriminated from cold dark matter with $2\sigma$ precision for a half-sky Euclid-like survey.
